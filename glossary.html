    Glossary Glossary

    abrasives.
    🔗
    Insoluble minerals used to polish and grind hard surfaces. Abrasives are used in toothpaste to clean the tooth enamel.
    🔗
    acetic acid.
    🔗
    An acid with the formula \(\ce{CH3COOH}\text{,}\) found in vinegar.
    🔗
    acid mantle.
    🔗
    An acidic surface barrier on the human skin with pH 4.5 to 5.5.
    🔗
    adsorption.
    🔗
    A process where molecules adhere themselves onto the exterior surface of solids as thin films.
    🔗
    alkaline.
    🔗
    Referring to a solution, having a pH higher than 7.
    🔗
    α-helices.
    🔗
    A secondary structure that proteins adopt. Looks like a coiled spring and can stretch and compress under moderate force.
    🔗
    amino acids.
    🔗
    The basic building blocks of proteins. Consists of an amino group, a carboxylic acid, and a variable side chain.
    🔗
    amphiphilic.
    🔗
    Referring to molecules, having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions.
    🔗
    amphoteric surfactant.
    🔗
    A surfactant where the head has both positive and negative regions.
    🔗
    anionic surfactants.
    🔗
    A negatively charged surfactant.
    🔗
    bent geometry.
    🔗
    The molecular geometry that some molecules like water adopt.
    🔗
    β-sheets.
    🔗
    A secondary structure that proteins adopt. Looks like highly ordered, flat sheets packed side-by-side. Found in silk and fibrous structures and resistant to stretching.
    🔗
    boil.
    🔗
    The process of turning a liquid into a gas through heat transfer.
    🔗
    boiling point.
    🔗
    The temperature at which something begins to boil. The boiling point of water is 100 degrees Celsius.
    🔗
    calcium carboxylate.
    🔗
    A mineral consisting of a positive calcium ion \(\ce{Ca^{2+}}\) and two negatively charged amphiphilic molecules.
    🔗
    capillary action.
    🔗
    A process, typically carried out by water, in which a fluid flows by itself in a narrow space without the help of external forces.
    🔗
    carboxylate.
    🔗
    A molecule consisting of a hydrocarbon chain connected to a negatively charged \(\ce{COO^-}\) group.
    🔗
    citric acid.
    🔗
    A weak organic acid found in lemons. Used in body wash to balance the pH to match the skin.
    🔗
    cocamidopropyl betaine.
    🔗
    An amphoteric surfactant commonly used in body wash.
    🔗
    cohesion.
    🔗
    The attraction between like molecules (as opposed to adhesion).
    🔗
    condense.
    🔗
    The process of turning a gas into a liquid through heat transfer.
    🔗
    conduction.
    🔗
    The transfer of heat through physical contact.
    🔗
    convection.
    🔗
    The transfer of heat through fluid flow.
    🔗
    core electrons.
    🔗
    The electrons in non-outermost shells of an atom. These typically do not participate in any chemistry.
    🔗
    corneocytes.
    🔗
    Cells in the stratum corneum.
    🔗
    cortex.
    🔗
    Referring to hair, the middle and most massive layer. Consists of keratin proteins that give rise to its structural rigidity.
    🔗
    covalent bonds.
    🔗
    A chemical bond between atoms where electron pairs are shared between them.
    🔗
    crown.
    🔗
    The part of the tooth that is exposed to air.
    🔗
    cuticle.
    🔗
    Referring to hair, the outermost layer. Consists of “scales” that can lie flat or lift.
    🔗
    cysteine.
    🔗
    An amino acid whose sidechain consists of a thiol ( \(\ce{-SH}\) ) group. Characterized by its ability to form disulfide bridges.
    🔗
    demineralization.
    🔗
    The breakdown of hydroxyapatite in the tooth enamel. The reverse of remineralization.
    🔗
    diffusion.
    🔗
    The spontaneous spreading of molecules from a concentrated region to a dilute region.
    🔗
    disulfide bridge.
    🔗
    A covalent bond that forms between two nearby cysteine molecules. Gives rise to an individual’s natural hair structure.
    🔗
    dynamic equilibrium.
    🔗
    During, “tug-of-war” between two reverse processes, a comfortable middle-ground where the two proceed at the same rate.
    🔗
    electron geometry.
    🔗
    The geometry of a molecule including its unbonded electron pairs.
    🔗
    electronegativity.
    🔗
    A number associated with an atom that describes how much it is attracted to electrons.
    🔗
    electrons.
    🔗
    Negatively charged subatomic particles, one of three that make up an atom.
    🔗
    emollients.
    🔗
    Molecules that slip into microscopic fissures in the skin, thereby softening and smoothening it.
    🔗
    emulsifier.
    🔗
    Amphiphilic molecules that reside between two substances and maintain emulsions.
    🔗
    emulsion.
    🔗
    In a solution, when small droplets of one substance is suspended throughout another.
    🔗
    enamel.
    🔗
    The outermost layer of the crown of the human tooth.
    🔗
    fluorapatite.
    🔗
    A mineral with formula \(\ce{Ca10(PO4)6F2}\text{.}\) Fluoride toothpaste replaces hydroxyapatite with the stronger fluorapatite.
