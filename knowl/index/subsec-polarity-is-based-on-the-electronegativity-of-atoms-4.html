When the electronegativity difference is low, the bond is nonpolar. This can happen in two ways. First, atoms share electrons to complete an octet — this is the covalent bond we are familiar with. Second, atoms let go of their electrons to form a “sea” of electrons that can move around — this is known as metallic bonding. Generally, covalent bonds form when it is easier to gain electrons to form an octet, whereas metallic bonds form when it is easier to lose electrons to empty the outermost electron shell.
