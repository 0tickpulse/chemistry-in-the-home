Example 2.5.2. Orange Juice and Toothpaste.

There is a common phenomenon that occurs as follows: apply toothpaste, and then drink orange juice. The taste is often utterly repulsive — why is that?
🔗
Solution.
As we will elaborate on in Chapter 3, the tongue contains taste receptors for different tastes, such as sweet and bitter. When toothpaste is introduced into the mouth, the SLS molecules temporarily numb the sweet receptors, and also break down the phospholipids that normally block the bitter receptors. As a result, the sweetness of orange juice is completely masked, and you only taste the raw bitterness.
