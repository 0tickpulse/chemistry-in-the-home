The primary mineral in the enamel is called hydroxyapatite. Similar to table salt ( \(\ce{NaCl}\) ), it is an ionic substance, consisting of a positive part and a negative part. In this case, the positive ion is calcium ( \(\ce{Ca^{2+}}\) ), and the negative ion is a combination of phosphate ( \(\ce{PO4^{3-}}\) ) and hydroxide ( \(\ce{OH-}\) ). The full formula for hydroxyapatite is \(\ce{Ca10(PO4)6(OH)2}\text{.}\)
