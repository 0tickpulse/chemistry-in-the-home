Example 2.5.1.

Soak one plain chicken eggshell in white vinegar. Coat a second eggshell in fluoride toothpaste for 10 minutes, wipe it clean, and submerge it in a separate cup of vinegar. What happens? Why?
🔗
Solution.
The untreated eggshell bubbles violently and softens. On the other hand, the fluoride-treated shell bubbles significantly slower and maintains its structural rigidity stronger.
🔗
Vinegar is made of an acid called acetic acid. When eggshell, made primarily of calcium carbonate ( \(\ce{CaCO3}\) ), is exposed to it, the acid dissolves them in a process similar to demineralization in teeth.
🔗
When toothpaste is introduced to the shell, the fluoride ions bind into the surface minerals, forming an acid-resistant barrier that slows down the process.
