The difference in electronegativity between two atoms is what accounts for the polarity of a covalent bond between them. Oxygen ( \(\chi = 3.44\) ) and hydrogen ( \(\chi = 2.20\) ) have a electronegativity difference of \(1.24\text{,}\) which is significant enough for \(\ce{O-H}\) to be considered a polar bond. If the electronegativity difference is high enough, such as in the case of sodium ( \(\chi = 0.93\) ) chloride ( \(\chi = 3.16\) ), we treat the bond as an ionic bond.
