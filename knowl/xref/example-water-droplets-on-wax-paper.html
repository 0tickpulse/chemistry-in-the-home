Checkpoint 1.1.3.

One easy way to show that water and oil differ a lot in their cohesion is as follows.
🔗
Grab a single drop of water and place it on wax paper. Do the same with a drop of cooking oil. You will notice that the water drop is much more tall and dome-shaped ( Figure 1.1.4 ). This is because the strong hydrogen bonds in the water make the waterdrop pull in on itself. This is called surface tension. In contrast, the oil drop spreads out flatly due to the absence of hydrogen bonds.
